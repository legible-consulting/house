# Seller kit — how to send me a lead

This kit is yours. It says how a lead reaches me, what to send, what to answer when they
hesitate, what I sell, and what your 10% looks like. Read the target profile first (who we
look for) and the cadence (three touches, then stop). The Pitch drafts are the full version
of each touch. The messages here are the short ones, for someone you already know. A marked
part is yours to fill. You send from your own email, phone or WhatsApp, or in person. The
desk sends nothing, and I never see the message you sent.

## From a name to a handoff

1. **Add the row before the first message.** On the pool page: the company, the person, an
   email or a phone number, the language, and the source (who referred them, which event,
   which list). The row is yours from then on. The email on the row also keeps the lead
   yours when they write to me through my site.
2. **Send the first touch and log it.** Date, channel, one line. The row moves to `contacted`
   and the desk sets the next date. A first email or WhatsApp to a business that did not ask
   goes to a company address, never to a person's own, and keeps the two lines at the end of
   the opener draft: where you found them, and that one reply stops it. A no is permanent:
   set `lost` with the reason `asked not to be contacted`, and never add that company again
   (the cadence).
3. **Follow the cadence.** A new angle on day 7, a last note on day 21. Log every touch. A
   reply makes the row `replied`. A meeting with you makes it `meeting`.
4. **Hand it to me.** Write what they asked for, in their words, as the ask. With the contact,
   an email or a phone number, the country, the language, the ask and one logged touch, set
   `ready for Jamie`. I read the handoff and take the deal from there. You see its stage up
   to the agreement.
5. **You are paid.** 10% of what I invoice under the first signed agreement, as the client
   pays. The table at the end shows what that looks like.

## Openers

Three ways in, one each. Pick the channel the person already uses with you.

### WhatsApp

> Hi <first name>. You mentioned <one thing seen> was eating your week. I know the right
> person. Jamie builds tools for businesses like yours: he takes the one task your team
> repeats, works out whether software or an assistant could carry it, and tells you straight
> what is worth building and what is not. Twenty minutes with him is enough to find out.
> Want me to set it up?

### Email

> Hi <first name>,
>
> Following up on what you said about <one thing seen>. I work with Jamie Nisbet, a software
> engineer in Mafra, near Lisbon. He starts with a short call about how your business runs,
> then builds what makes sense at a fixed price. The scope is agreed before any work starts,
> and the price does not move after that.
>
> If twenty minutes with him would be useful, say so and I will introduce you directly.

### In person

Say it, then text it the same day:

> "I know exactly the right person for that. Jamie builds websites and software, one person
> end to end, and he quotes straight. Let me put you two in touch."

Then the WhatsApp opener, so the thread exists and you can log it.

## Follow-ups

Three touches, then stop. Each one is a different angle, not a repeat. The desk shows the
next date on the row.

### Day 7 · a new angle

> Hi <first name>, one more thought on <one thing seen>. Jamie did something similar for
> <example>: a task done by hand every week, turned into a tool the team runs on its own,
> with a person still checking the unusual cases. Would twenty minutes next week suit?

### Day 21 · the last note

> Hi <first name>, this is my last note on this. If the timing is wrong, or <one thing seen>
> is not the problem it looked like, that is a fair answer. If it becomes relevant later,
> this thread is the place. Reply whenever suits.

### When they go quiet

Seven days after the last note, the row is due for `lost`. Set it, with the reason. A new
approach later is a new row that names this one in its source.

### When they reply

The row becomes `replied`. Ask what they want done, in their words. That is the ask. Offer
twenty minutes with me, or meet them yourself first. Then set `ready for Jamie`.

## If they hesitate

**"It's too expensive."** Nobody has a price yet. I price after the call, as a fixed price
with two options: the smallest honest version that does the job, and the whole scope. The
price does not move once agreed. If the price pressure comes before anyone has understood
the work, let it go: set `lost` with that reason.

**"I'll just do it myself on Wix."** They can, and sometimes that is the right call. The
difference is the weekends spent on a template that still looks like one. I build it once,
properly, on their own domain, and they get their time back. If they enjoy doing it
themselves, do not push.

**"I already know someone."** No hard sell. I am one engineer who takes the whole thing,
scope to handover, at a fixed price. Leave the door open for when the first option goes
quiet. One check: if two developers have already left the same project, let it go. A third
contractor on a haunted project is what ends a lead.

**"Not right now."** Fine. Do not chase. Ask what would make it the right time: a launch, a
busy season, a new brand. Put that date on the row and come back then.

**"What if it goes wrong? I'm not technical."** They never touch the technical side. One
person scopes it, builds it and hands it over. They see a preview before every release and
say yes to it before it goes live. At the end they get a handover pack, and support if they
want it. You are not on the hook for any of it either.

## What makes a lead worth sending

The more of these, the better:

- **Someone vouched.** You, or a person they trust. Trust exists before the first call.
- **A real operating business.** Customers and revenue today. The work serves something
  that already runs.
- **One decider who pays fast.** One person says yes. The deposit arrives without chasing.
- **A process done by hand today.** A task a person repeats every week that software could
  carry. This is the lead I chase first.
- **A reason it is now.** A launch, a busy season, a new brand, a competitor who just moved
  online.

Before I take it, the row carries: the person's name, an email or a phone number, the
country, the language, the ask in their words, and one logged touch.

Either of these ends a lead. Set `lost` with the reason:

- Haggling before anyone has scoped the work.
- Two developers already left the same project.

An idea with no customers yet does not end a lead on its own. I decide those on the call.

## What I sell

| Line | The client gets |
|---|---|
| Site | Their business findable and presentable: pages, a contact, booking or menu flow, on their own domain, live and handed over. |
| App | A working tool with state: accounts, data, an admin view, the workflows their business runs on. |
| Platform | An app with several roles or several surfaces: a marketplace, a product with its admin, docs and support. |
| Workshop | One day, on site or remote, that teaches a team my way of organising work so that software and assistants can carry it. The materials stay with them. |
| Coaching | One-to-one hours in which each person applies that way of working to their own job. |
| Retainer | Ongoing work against a written monthly scope. Hosting and basic support ride inside it. |

Three rules:

- **You never quote.** Every price is mine, said on a call or in a proposal, never in a
  message. The budget hint on the row is a hint, nothing more.
- **Fixed price or monthly retainer.** I do not sell hours.
- **The words are theirs.** Page copy and translations are the client's to bring, or their
  own line in the proposal.

A lead that fits no line is still worth a row. Say what they asked for and I decide.

## What your 10% looks like

10% of what I invoice under the first signed agreement of a deal you sourced, net of VAT,
hosting and third-party costs. It is owed as the client pays, in proportion to each paid
invoice. Nothing is owed on an unpaid invoice. Later work, and retainer months after that
first agreement, pay nothing. The monthly close lists what is owed, and I settle it against
the payments.

| The work | The client pays | You earn |
|---|---|---|
| A site | €1,500 | €150 |
| A site with real integrations | €4,000 | €400 |
| An app | €12,000 | €1,200 |
| A retainer, three months agreed at €2,000 a month | €6,000 over the three months | €600, as each month is paid |

Examples only. Your 10% is always 10% of what the client actually pays under that first
agreement.
