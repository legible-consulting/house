# Seller kit — how to send me a lead

I am Jamie. This kit is everything you need to send me a lead. First, who I am and what I
sell. Then the system, from the row you add to the 10% you earn. Last, the words to send and
the answers to give. Read it once in order, then come back to a lesson when you need it.

You send every message from your own email, phone or WhatsApp, or you say it in person. The
desk sends nothing, and I never see the message you sent. A marked part, like
<first name>, is yours to fill.

## Who I am

- key: who-jamie-is
- part: jamie

I am Jamie Nisbet, a software engineer in Mafra, near Lisbon. I have worked this way since
2021, for businesses in Portugal, Belgium, the UK, the UAE and the United States.

I build sites, apps and platforms for businesses. I teach teams a way of organising their
work so that software and assistants can carry part of it. And I give a second opinion on
where the tools fit and where they do not.

I am one person. I take the call, scope the work, build it and hand it over. Nobody is passed
down a chain.

I work in English, French and Portuguese. Every client reads their own language.

What I do not do:

- Copywriting, branding or design as the job itself. I point people at someone who does it
  well.
- Hourly work. Every job is a fixed price or a written monthly scope.

## How I work

- key: how-jamie-works
- part: jamie

It is the same every time, so you can promise it.

1. **A call first.** Twenty minutes about how their business runs and what they want done. I
   tell them straight what is worth building and what is not.
2. **A fixed price with two options.** After the call I write a proposal: the smallest honest
   version that does the job, and the whole scope. The price is its last section.
3. **The scope is agreed before the work starts.** After that, the price does not move. A
   lower price takes a named piece of the scope with it.
4. **A preview before every release.** They see each change before it goes live, and they say
   yes to it.
5. **A handover pack at the end.** What they have, how to use it, who hosts what. Support if
   they want it.

I do not sell hours. And the words are theirs: page copy and translations are the client's to
bring, or their own line in the proposal.

## What I sell

- key: what-jamie-sells
- part: jamie

Fourteen lines, in five groups. On the row, pick the closest line as the service, or
`unknown`.

### Build

- **Site.** Their business findable and presentable: pages, a contact, booking or menu flow,
  on their own domain, live and handed over.
- **Landing pages, fully serviced.** One page, designed, written, hosted and kept current by
  me. The copy is part of this one.
- **App.** A working tool with state: accounts, data, an admin view, the workflows their
  business runs on.
- **Platform.** An app with several roles or several surfaces: a marketplace, a product with
  its admin, docs and support.
- **Software engineering.** Web apps and systems built to last: fast, accessible, documented
  and easy to change.
- **GEO optimisation.** Their business cited in the answers of ChatGPT, Gemini and
  Perplexity, not only in search results.

### Teach

- **Workshop.** One day, on site or remote, that teaches a team my way of organising work so
  that software and assistants can carry it. The materials stay with them, with an attendance
  record they can file.
- **Coaching.** One-to-one hours in which each person applies that way of working to their
  own job.

### Advise

- **AI consultancy.** A second opinion on where the tools fit and where they do not: the few
  uses worth doing, and the rest named and skipped.
- **Project management.** Someone technical who keeps a build on track: the scope, the
  timeline and the people on it, with honest status they can act on.

### Automate

- **AI & non-AI automation.** The repeated steps of their week done by software: their tools
  connected, a model where it earns its place, plain code where it does not.
- **AI business infrastructure.** The base that makes an assistant useful across their
  business: their own documents, searchable, and answers grounded in their data.
- **Lead-gen channel development.** The channels that bring work in: referrals, outreach and
  the tooling behind them.

### Retainer

- **Retainer.** Ongoing work against a written monthly scope. Hosting and basic support ride
  inside it.

Three rules:

- **You never quote.** Every price is mine, said on a call or in a proposal, never in a
  message. The budget on the row is a hint, nothing more.
- **Fixed price or monthly retainer.** I do not sell hours.
- **The words are theirs.** Page copy and translations are the client's to bring, or their
  own line in the proposal. A fully serviced landing page is the one exception.

Say "AI" only after they do. With a café owner the word gets in the way. With a tech company
it is their own word.

A lead that fits no line is still worth a row. Write what they asked for and I decide.

## Who we look for

- key: who-we-look-for
- part: jamie

Two kinds of business:

- **A local business.** A café, a venue, a practice, a shop, with one owner. Sites and small
  apps. Most of these come to me by referral.
- **A company with a process done by hand.** A task someone repeats every week that software
  could carry. Bigger work, often ongoing, often remote. This is the lead I want first.

In Portugal, Belgium and the UK, in Portuguese, French or English. Another country is fine
for remote work when they read one of the three. Every message goes in their language.

A good lead shows some of these. The more, the better:

- **Someone vouched.** You, or a person they trust. Trust exists before the first call.
- **A real operating business.** Customers and revenue today.
- **One decider who pays fast.** One person says yes, and the deposit arrives without
  chasing.
- **A process done by hand today.** The task someone repeats every week.
- **A reason it is now.** A launch, a busy season, a new brand, a competitor who just moved
  online.

Either of these ends a lead. Set `lost` with the reason:

- Haggling on price before anyone has scoped the work.
- Two developers already left the same project. A third will not fix it.

An idea with no customers yet does not end a lead on its own. I decide those on the call.

## Add the row

- key: add-the-row
- part: system
- status: new
- next: Send the first touch from your own account, then log it on the row.

Add the row before you send the first message. The row keeps the lead yours. Its email also
keeps it yours if they write to me through my site.

1. **Search the pool first.** One company has one row. If another seller has it, it is
   theirs. If a row says `asked not to be contacted`, that company is never added again.
2. **Now: the company and the source.** The source is how you found them: who referred them,
   which event, which list.
3. **Before you hand it to me: the rest.** The person's name, an email or a phone number, the
   country, the language, and what they asked for.

The desk makes the slug from the company name: the row's short name, like `cafe-central`. You
can change it until the handoff. The desk refuses a slug that is already in the pool or is
already one of my clients.

The row starts at `new`.

## The first touch

- key: the-first-touch
- part: system
- status: contacted
- touch: 1
- next: Wait for a reply, and send the next touch on the date the row shows.

Send the first message from your own email, phone or WhatsApp, or say it in person. Start from
the draft for touch 1 and make it yours.

When they did not ask to hear from us:

- **Email or WhatsApp goes to the company.** `info@`, `geral@`, the switchboard, the shop's
  own WhatsApp. Never a person's own address or number.
- **A sole trader, or a person's own number: call or visit first.** Email and WhatsApp open
  once they engage.
- **Keep the two lines at the end of the draft.** Where you found them, and that one reply
  stops it. You fill in where.

Then log the touch on the row: the date, the channel, one line. The row moves to `contacted`,
and the desk sets the next date, seven days on.

## Follow the cadence

- key: follow-the-cadence
- part: system
- touch: 2 | 3

Three touches, then stop. Each one is a new angle, not a repeat.

- **Day 7: a new angle.** One example of similar work, or one concrete idea.
- **Day 21: the last note.** Say that it is the last one, and leave the door open.

Log every touch. The desk moves the next date each time.

**When they go quiet.** On day 28, seven days after the last note, the row is due for `lost`.
Set it, with the reason. A new try later is a new row that names this one in its source.

**When they say no.** Press "They asked not to be contacted" on the row. It is for good: no
seller contacts that company again, on any channel, and the desk refuses its email and its
number.

## When they reply

- key: when-they-reply
- part: system
- status: replied
- next: Write what they want, in their words, as the ask, and offer twenty minutes with me.

Set the row to `replied`. The cadence stops here. From now on you set the next date yourself.

Ask what they want done, in their words, and write it on the row as the ask. One paragraph is
enough. It is the first thing I read.

Then offer twenty minutes with me. Or meet them yourself first, if that suits them better.

## Set up the meeting

- key: set-up-the-meeting
- part: system
- status: meeting
- next: After the meeting, fill in what I need and set ready for Jamie.

My booking link, Discovery Call, is under this lesson. Send it to them from your own account,
with one line about who I am. They pick a time that suits them, and the booking comes to me. If
no link shows under this lesson, ask me for it.

When they have booked, set the row to `meeting`. The move asks for the date and the time, in
Lisbon time, who takes the meeting, you or me, and where: a place, a phone number or a link.
The meetings booked with me show on my own page of the desk. A meeting with you counts too: if
you meet them yourself first, set `meeting` as well, with you as the one who takes it. If the
time moves, change it on the row.

On the call I listen: how their business runs, what they want done, what done looks like. I
tell them straight what is worth building and what is not. The price comes after the call, in
a proposal, as a fixed price with two options. You are welcome on the call, and you do not
need to be there.

## Hand it to me

- key: hand-it-to-jamie
- part: system
- status: ready for Jamie
- next: Wait for me to take it, then press "Check the PR" on the row.

Once they have replied, or you have met, hand the lead to me. The row needs:

- the person's name
- an email or a phone number
- the country and the language
- the ask, in their words
- at least one logged touch

You also need a handle, the short name the desk knows you by. If you have none, ask me.

Set `ready for Jamie`. The desk then opens a pull request: my request to open the client's
folder from your row. I read it and accept it. That is the handoff.

Then press "Check the PR" on the row. If I took the lead, the row moves to `handed off`. If I
did not, it goes back to where it was, and I write why in the notes.

## After the handoff

- key: after-the-handoff
- part: system
- status: handed off
- next: Nothing to do on the row. Watch the deal's stage.

The row is read-only now. The client has a folder of their own, and the deal is mine.

You see where it stands, up to the agreement:

- `prospect`: I know who they are.
- `research`: I learn their trade before the call or the proposal.
- `proposal`: I wrote the proposal, with the price as its last section, and they are reading
  it.
- `agreement`: they said yes, and the agreement is being signed.

After that you see no more stages and no documents. When the agreement is signed, I write down
your 10%.

## Your 10%

- key: your-10-percent
- part: system

10% of what I invoice under the first signed agreement of a deal you sourced, net of VAT,
hosting and third-party costs.

- **As the client pays.** It is owed in proportion to each paid invoice. Nothing is owed on an
  unpaid invoice.
- **The first agreement only.** Later work, and retainer months after that first agreement,
  pay nothing.
- **Every month.** The monthly close lists what is owed, and I settle it against the
  payments.

| The work | The client pays | You earn |
|---|---|---|
| A site | €1,500 | €150 |
| A site with real integrations | €4,000 | €400 |
| An app | €12,000 | €1,200 |
| A retainer, three months agreed at €2,000 a month | €6,000 over the three months | €600, as each month is paid |

Examples only. Your 10% is always 10% of what the client actually pays under that first
agreement.

## If they hesitate

- key: if-they-hesitate
- part: reference

**"It's too expensive."** Nobody has a price yet. I price after the call, as a fixed price
with two options: the smallest honest version that does the job, and the whole scope. The
price does not move once agreed. If the price pressure comes before anyone has understood
the work, let it go: set `lost` with that reason.

**"I'll just do it myself on Wix."** They can, and sometimes that is the right call. The
difference is the weekends spent on a template that still looks like one. I build it once,
properly, on their own domain, and they get their time back. If they enjoy doing it
themselves, do not push.

**"I already know someone."** No hard sell. I am one engineer who takes the whole thing,
scope to handover, at a fixed price. Leave the door open for when the first option goes
quiet. One check: if two developers have already left the same project, let it go. A third
contractor on a haunted project is what ends a lead.

**"Not right now."** Fine. Do not chase. Ask what would make it the right time: a launch, a
busy season, a new brand. Put that date on the row and come back then.

**"What if it goes wrong? I'm not technical."** They never touch the technical side. One
person scopes it, builds it and hands it over. They see a preview before every release and
say yes to it before it goes live. At the end they get a handover pack, and support if they
want it. You are not on the hook for any of it either.
