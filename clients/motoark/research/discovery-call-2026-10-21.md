**MotoArk Business Model and Market Tiers**: Sam Cherry described MotoArk as operating like Amazon for car parts buyers and like eBay or Amazon marketplace for car parts sellers. Sam Cherry explained that the regional market contains three tiers of part sales, focusing on the Gulf, Africa, and Southeast Asia. Tier one consists of traditional garages and dealerships like Toyota and Lexus, tier two comprises sub-dealers, and the bottom tier encompasses a massive pyramid including 5,000 garages in the United Arab Emirates alone ([<u>00:08:56</u>](#section-3)). Sam Cherry noted that 10 to 15 percent of these bottom-tier garages hold over \$50 million each in inventory, and MotoArk exclusively services this bottom tier with a mix of very few new parts and a massive amount of used parts in varying conditions ([<u>00:09:54</u>](#section-4)).

**Seller Operations and Inventory Management**: Sam Cherry detailed that many sellers buy crash-damaged vehicles, such as a flipped Lamborghini, and use inexpensive local labor to strip salvageable parts ([<u>00:10:45</u>](#section-5)). Sam Cherry noted employing 50 workers who run around in the heat to conduct full inventory counts and organize messy spaces at seller locations ([<u>00:11:32</u>](#section-6)). To professionalize this process, Sam Cherry stated that MotoArk charges sellers a 25 percent commission fee on all sales while charging buyers no platform fees ([<u>00:10:45</u>](#section-5)). In exchange for the commission, Sam Cherry explained that MotoArk uploads the items onto the platform, manages initial listings, and handles fulfillment ([<u>00:11:32</u>](#section-6)).

**Fulfillment Operations and Development Team Scale**: Sam Cherry explained that MotoArk manages delivery and fulfillment using local bicycle or motorbike couriers and provides a buy-now-pay-later option covering four months of cash flow for buyers ([<u>00:12:17</u>](#section-7)). Discussing the technical infrastructure, Sam Cherry noted that despite the massive scale, the current development team consists only of Sam Cherry as CTO and two offshore developers in Pakistan, operating on a total burn rate of less than \$3,000 per month ([<u>00:13:10</u>](#section-8)). **Technical Requirements and Agentic AI Integration**: Sam Cherry stated that they have brought the platform to version one but need to hire a senior engineer with an agentic-native approach to AI, moving the project from version one to version two over a three-month initial program ([<u>00:14:03</u>](#section-9)) ([<u>00:16:40</u>](#section-12)). Sam Cherry highlighted a critical business risk, noting that if they were hit by a bus, MotoArk would be left in jeopardy because the entire operation and software build depend heavily on their personal oversight ([<u>00:15:49</u>](#section-11)). Regarding platform capabilities, Sam Cherry explained that version one uses basic search indexing, but future plans include a WhatsApp-style interface powered by AI to support multilingual users speaking English, Hindi, and Arabic, alongside cross-border triage moving parts from high-supply regions like Morocco to high-demand areas using carrier agreements with DHL ([<u>00:18:16</u>](#section-14)).

**Next Steps, Availability, and Pricing Structure**: Sam Cherry requested that Jamie Nisbet provide examples of their past work via a video demonstration showing their transition from design to agentic build, testing, and DevOps ([<u>00:20:02</u>](#section-16)).
