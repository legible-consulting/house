# LESSON 2.2: Raise the Bar

*The Archive › Module 2: Old But Gold · Author: Jake Van Clief · Source: recorded video (link on Skool page) with written companion · Length: 24:44 (December 2023) · Link: https://youtu.be/iY_j0VKimQI*

## Summary

Jake argues that the right response to AI in education is not detection and prevention but a higher standard. If a student can paste a prompt into a chatbot and pass, the assignment is the problem. He supports this with a pattern: each new technology (calculators, the internet, AI) lifted the baseline of what is expected, and old expert skills became the new minimum. The companion adds that "raise the bar" is only half an answer: learners also need support and method to work above the new baseline.

## Core concepts

- **Technology stacking raises the baseline:** calculators raised the maths baseline, the internet the research baseline, and AI the writing and analysis baseline. Each time, the previous expert skill became the expected entry skill.
- **If AI can do it, the task is too easy:** assignments that tested recall or generic synthesis are the ones AI completes; judgment, perspective and context-specific application hold up.
- **Expertise moves down the curriculum:** the linguistics-professor story shows her advanced material becoming the starting point for freshmen while her top-tier work pushes higher.
- **Coding pyramid:** one line of high-level code controls thousands of lines of machine code, so LLMs are framed as the next layer up. The companion says this later grew into 60/30/10 orchestration.
- **Review as the assignment:** learners generate three AI essays from a prompt, then grade, rewrite and compare them. This teaches editing and critical reading and makes the cheating debate moot.
- **Generic writing becomes visible:** heavy AI users learn to spot lazy AI output, so trained human judgment beats detectors.

## Frameworks and methods

The review-assignment pattern from the page's exercise:

1. Pick a familiar task and define three review criteria.
2. Generate or supply three short drafts.
3. Have the learner explain one correction per draft.
4. Check whether the explanation shows the skill you intended to teach.

## Claims and evidence

- AI detectors are unreliable and an arms race is unwinnable — asserted in the companion — widely discussed but no source given on the page.
- Peer-reviewed studies show AI use improves student writing — video says links were provided — not captured here, unverified.
- "Just make it harder" fails without support — the companion's own correction.
