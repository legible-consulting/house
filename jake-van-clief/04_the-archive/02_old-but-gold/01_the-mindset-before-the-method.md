# LESSON 2.1: The Mindset Before the Method

*The Archive › Module 2: Old But Gold · Author: Jake Van Clief · Source: recorded workshop video (link on Skool page) with written companion · Length: 39:25 (January 2024) · Link: https://youtu.be/czIBNYeiAuw*

## Summary

Recorded after a workshop for college professors, this video argues that AI in education is a mindset problem rather than a tool problem. The fundamentals of teaching did not change; the baseline everyone starts from rose, so personal expertise and perspective become more valuable, not less. Jake diagnoses three problems (overwhelm, poor tools, no serious community), makes a case that you can bring AI into a classroom without touching a chatbot, and ends with his own THINK writing process. The later companion revisits the talk, keeping the thesis but flagging outdated tools and over-emphasis on prompting.

## Core concepts

- **AI as a mindset problem:** decide what learning or work outcome you want before choosing any tool. The page's reading guide frames this as defining what good reasoning looks like first.
- **Baseline rises, expertise matters more:** when everyone can get the same base output, the individual expert's perspective is the scarce asset.
- **Thinking before tooling:** you can teach the history and implications of thinking machines without opening a chatbot. Useful for resistant audiences.
- **The PhD thought experiment:** if an AI wrote a dissertation and a board approved it, is the issue the tool or the system? If the board rejected it, human judgment still matters. It separates emotional reaction from the structural question.
- **Calculator analogy:** teachers learned when to allow and restrict calculators; AI use should be taught the same way, as something with intended and unintended uses.
- **Three problems:** overwhelm and fear of replacement, tools that are hard or consumer-grade, and no serious educator community. The companion says each became a product line.

## Frameworks and methods

THINK, Jake's early academic-writing workflow (as named in the companion):

1. Thoughts to Thematics: dictate rough ideas, let AI organise them into themes.
2. Heuristic Helper: use AI to suggest structure or approach.
3. Integrate Data: bring in sources and research.
4. Navigational Nemesis: ask AI to challenge, find bias or counterpoints.
5. Knowledge Amplification: have AI explain concepts back to you to deepen understanding.

The companion calls this "Layer 0 thinking", an early seed of the folder architecture and context hierarchy taught now.

- AI "raised the price of expertise" — companion cites 600+ trained professionals at named firms and 12,500+ community members — self-reported, unverified.
- Prompt engineering as the central skill — the companion explicitly retracts the emphasis: prompting is the least important layer compared with architecture and layer choice.
- F-35 ejection story — companion says it is closer to advanced autopilot than modern language models.
- Per the companion, 60/30/10 and orchestration frameworks did not exist at recording time.
