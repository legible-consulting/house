# LESSON 2.3: More Human

*The Archive › Module 2: Old But Gold · Author: Jake Van Clief · Source: recorded video (link on Skool page) with written companion · Length: 27:54 (December 2023) · Link: https://youtu.be/R-73VRNTLaY*

## Summary

Jake opens with a personal story from 2019: climbing Mount Fuji during a typhoon, finding an injured hiker and spending hours helping him to a shrine. He uses it to argue that unrepeatable lived experience is what makes people human and cannot be automated. He then reads his own response article to a newspaper piece about an AI-assisted PhD, arguing that AI removes the robotic parts of work and exposes where systems were already formulaic. The companion says the story and the "AI is making us more human" claim are this lesson's distinct contribution; much else repeats 2.1 and 2.2.

## Core concepts

- **AI removes the robotic, not the human:** formulaic, bureaucratic tasks are automated first; judgment, context and decisions under ambiguity remain.
- **AI as a mirror:** if a model can pass your review process, that reveals something about the process. AI shows where work was already mechanical.
- **Scribes and the printing press:** the press handled scale while scribes specialised in meaning. By analogy, the model handles scale and the human handles meaning.
- **Value in questioning and guiding:** a good prompt reflects the author's expertise, so the human role is to question, probe and guide.
- **Back-in-my-day syndrome:** dismissing new methods as lesser. The companion softens this: fear usually reflects genuine uncertainty, not defensiveness.
- **Replication does not diminish originals:** art and engineering analogies argue that reusing techniques does not erase value.

## Frameworks and methods

The companion describes a consulting sequence that starts from this idea:

1. Map the workflows.
2. Find the robotic parts.
3. Decide what to automate and what to protect.

The page exercise adapts it: list one routine step an assistant could help with, one decision you keep, and the evidence required before approving output.

## Claims and evidence

- Validated across sectors — companion cites 600+ trained professionals at named firms — self-reported, unverified.
- The Fuji narrative — first-person anecdote, not independently checkable, and not tied to AI evidence.
- Repeated material (PhD thought experiment, calculator analogy, "AI like Google") — companion confirms overlap with 2.1 and 2.2; about half the video is a verbatim article reading.
