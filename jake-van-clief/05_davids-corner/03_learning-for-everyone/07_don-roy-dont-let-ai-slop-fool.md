# Don Roy - Don't Let AI Slop Fool You

*David's Corner › Learning for Everyone · Author: Don Roy (essay), with a closing note from David's Corner · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Don Roy's essay separates low-effort AI output from useful work. He argues slop comes from one-line prompts posted unedited under volume-rewarding incentives, and that it reflects how people use the tools, not what the tools can do. He says the gap between careless and careful AI work is widening and that people who build regularly, even with fictional clients, will be better placed. David's closing note frames the difference as taste, discernment and skill. The exercise is to take one fictional client deliverable, check three factual claims, and cut anything that does not help the reader act.

## Core concepts

- **Slop is a human-in-the-loop failure:** the problem is removing judgement and optimising for clicks.
- **Amplifier view:** AI magnifies both weak and strong work.
- **Practice as moat:** regular building and learning to look past noise is the advantage, and the window may not stay open.
- **Edit test:** compare an edited deliverable against the first draft.
