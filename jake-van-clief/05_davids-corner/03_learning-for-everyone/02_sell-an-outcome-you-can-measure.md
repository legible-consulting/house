# Sell an Outcome You Can Measure

*David's Corner › Learning for Everyone · Author: David's Corner (author not stated; draws on nonprofit and early client work) · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

The lesson argues that value should be stated as a measurable result before any talk of models, agents or hours. It proposes a five-part outcome brief covering the current work, a baseline, a target over a trial period, quality conditions that must hold, and an owner. It separates build cost, running cost and support in the offer, and treats fixed, retainer and performance structures as options, provided both sides can verify the measure. A fictional recap example shows a proposed trial target, explicitly not a promised saving.

## Core concepts

- **Baseline includes corrections:** time per accepted result, not per first draft.
- **Quality guard:** the target counts only if stated quality conditions still hold.
- **Unbundled offer:** build, running costs and support priced apart.
- **Verifiable measure:** pick a metric both sides can check; revenue and profit depend on outside factors.
- **Tools are options:** the open-source stacks tried are not evidence of a pricing model.

## Apply to icm-workshop

- **S3 proposal:** adapt the five-field brief as the proposal's problem/approach frame, giving a client "a document they understand without a second call".
- **S4 quote:** the three-way split matches S4's economics record (hours, model cost, hosting, margin) and a fixed or retainer choice; keep the negotiation floor private.
