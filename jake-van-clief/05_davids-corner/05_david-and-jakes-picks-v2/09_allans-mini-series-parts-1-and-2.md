# Allan's Mini-Series (parts 1&2)

*David's Corner › David and Jake's Picks V2 · Author: Allan (member; posts summarised by David's Corner) · Source: text lesson (links to two original posts)*

## Summary

Allan's two-part case begins with repetitive portal corrections done by an assistant observing the browser, hits token limits, then moves to a scripted keyboard approach; the page says part 2 reports large usage and speed gains in that environment. The exercise maps a harmless workflow into read, propose, review and apply steps, finds the repeated action a script could do, notes the page state it depends on, and sets a stop condition, all on fictional records. The caution: a script repeats mistakes as well as instructions, and success on one portal says nothing about others.

## Core concepts

- **Observation versus script:** a script is cheaper when the action repeats and the page is predictable.
- **Four-step shape:** read, propose, review, apply.
- **Stop condition and action count** are checked, not assumed.
- **Compare time and cost** on the same task.

## Claims and evidence

- Large token and time savings — member-reported in part 2 — anecdote, specific to that portal.

## Apply to icm-workshop

- Weak relevance to the goals (no portal automation in scope). The read, propose, review, apply shape is reusable for D3 hosting records or any bulk change across the estate that D4 might one day fix: report first, human approves, then apply.
