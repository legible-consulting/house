# The Mirror and the Window

*David's Corner › Curtis Hays - Systems Worth Amplifying · Author: Curtis Hays · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Curtis argues that a company's purpose cannot be declared in a workshop; it has to be found by listening to customers. He recounts an agency exercise that produced a tidy but inert statement, then contrasts it with his co-host's method of turning an inward-looking mirror into a window onto the customer. He applies the same idea to AI: a system prompt that merely asserts a persona is a declared posture, whereas a governance document grounded in customer evidence and real corrections holds up when situations are new. The learner should leave with a test for any positioning claim: what customer evidence supports it, and what is assumption.

## Core concepts

- **Declared versus found:** a statement written from introspection looks right but shapes no decisions; a statement built from customer language does.
- **Mirror to window:** the discipline is to stop asking what the brand believes and go to the people who buy and take their words as the source.
- **Posture versus worldview in AI:** a generic helpful-assistant instruction is asserted, copied and inert; a doctrine built from actual decisions and repeated corrections has something to protect when instructions run out.
- **Months before minutes:** an agent built in fifteen minutes only works because of months of belief work that preceded it; the sequence does not reverse.
- **Scars as context:** models know textbook theory but have not been burned; the useful input is the lessons that cost something.
- **Excavation not invention:** keyword research is the same move, reading back the language of people who already want something.
- **Convergence as support:** strangers arriving at the same structure independently is offered as evidence that the pattern is discovered rather than invented.

## Frameworks and methods

The argument, in order:

1. A Why declared in a room reads fine and changes nothing.
2. Reason: it is a mirror, rearranging existing assumptions.
3. The alternative is a window: go to customers and use their words as the source.
4. The same failure appears in AI when builders type a posture into the system prompt because it sounds right.
5. The better route is to let character and doctrine emerge from real decisions and corrections, then record it.
6. For clients, a governing document holds positioning, language and the customer-validated reason people buy, with research done outward.
7. The months of discovery come first and the engineering second.
8. Conclusion: solve for the Why rather than declare it.
