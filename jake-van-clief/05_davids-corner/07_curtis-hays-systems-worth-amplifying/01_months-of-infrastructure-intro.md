# 8 Months of Infrastructure (Intro)

*David's Corner › Curtis Hays - Systems Worth Amplifying · Author: Curtis Hays · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Curtis Hays opens his guest series by explaining where his thinking comes from: a long career in IT and networks, followed by over a decade in paid media and consulting, where he came to treat client work as one system rather than a string of campaigns. His thesis is that AI is best understood as a way to protect and scale judgment, and that the one thing nobody can copy is the reason you are building in the first place. The page works as a table of contents for the series and a prompt to write down the judgment you currently carry in your head.

## Core concepts

- **Perception gap:** synthetic content (cloned voices, faceless avatars, flawless but empty posts) is already hard to distinguish from the real thing, and builders are both producing and consuming it.
- **AI as a judgment system:** rather than magic or plain infrastructure, AI is a place for an existing system of decisions to live and scale.
- **Belief as the moat:** prompts, skills and workspaces can all be cloned; the underlying conviction about why you build is the only part that stays yours. You can train a model on what works or on what you believe.
- **The question under the question:** beyond asking what problem you are solving (Jake's framing), Curtis asks why that problem matters to you at all. AI solves problems well but cannot rank which ones deserve solving.

## Frameworks and methods

1. Pick one recurring decision from your current work.
2. Name the problem it addresses and who benefits if it is solved.
3. Attach one real example that shows why it matters to you.
4. Finish with the judgment an assistant should preserve.
5. Result: a single short decision note that someone else could read before starting the task.

## Apply to icm-workshop

- **C1 shared core:** the "decision note" exercise is a cheap template for the identity/house-rules layer: each rule carries a problem, a beneficiary, an example and the judgment to preserve. That fits the goals' own boundary that a rule carries its own why and date. Adapt it as a short front-matter block for rule files in the shared core rather than a separate artefact.
- **O4 training and coaching:** the one-page decision note is a good opening exercise in a workshop kit, before any folder structure is introduced. Adopt as an exercise, not as system architecture.
