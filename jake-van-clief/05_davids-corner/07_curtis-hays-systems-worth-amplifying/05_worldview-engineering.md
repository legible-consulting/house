# Worldview Engineering

*David's Corner › Curtis Hays - Systems Worth Amplifying · Author: Curtis Hays · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Curtis predicts that prompting will become a commodity skill within a few years, as website building did before it, and that the durable edge moves to a layer underneath: what a system believes before it does any work. He calls the discipline of encoding that layer worldview engineering. The essay contrasts a belief layer with the thin be-helpful-and-concise posture found in most governance files, argues that borrowed scaffolds import someone else's convictions, and describes how he surfaced his own by mining transcripts. It closes on a historical quote about capturing a thinker's worldview in a machine. The learner is meant to write down their own tradeoff rules before building further.

## Core concepts

- **Upstream drift:** every time a capability becomes table stakes (websites, then SEO, social, content), the differentiator shifts to the layer beneath it. Prompting is next.
- **Worldview as lens, not rulebook:** a rulebook is finite and fails on unforeseen cases, whereas a worldview determines what the system perceives at all, so unanticipated cases are still read through the same frame.
- **Posture versus worldview:** a posture tells the system how to behave; a worldview supplies a defended definition of quality, forbidden statements, tradeoff priorities, an emotional register and a notion of originality, so it still guides when instructions run out.
- **Borrowed scaffolds smuggle beliefs:** copying someone's governance files imports value-laden choices that look like engineering. The risk is not breakage but a system that works while quietly serving another person's convictions.
- **Conviction comes from consequence:** AI has training data, which is other people's experience averaged, not a biography. It can scale judgment but not originate lived belief.
- **Customer-sourced truth:** a worldview is closer to what the customer has repeatedly said about you than what you believe about yourself.

## Frameworks and methods

The argument, in order:

1. Skills commoditise; the differentiator moves up a layer each time.
2. For AI, that layer is the system's beliefs, not better prompts.
3. Define a worldview as the frame you see through, then build governance as a lens rather than a list.
4. When two operators share identical models and architecture, the one with a belief layer produces distinctive work.
5. Typical governance files hold only a posture; a real belief layer carries quality, taboos, tradeoffs, register and originality.
6. Copying another builder's stack imports their convictions, so write your own.
7. Sequence of the work: mine your own conversations for patterns, write the actual rules, encode them in a layer loaded before every agent and session, and do this before building the folder structure.
8. At the far end, builders design how truth is evaluated, and those systems compound in coherence.
