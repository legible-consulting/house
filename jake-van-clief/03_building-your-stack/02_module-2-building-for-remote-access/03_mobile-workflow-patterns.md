# 2.3 Mobile Workflow Patterns

*Building Your Stack › Module 2: Building for Remote Access · Author: Jake Van Clief · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

A phone is good for small decisions, not big diffs or detailed visual review. The lesson names three useful patterns (check, decide, capture) and practises a desk-to-phone handoff with a deliberately small, read-only task that stops at an agreed point. On return, the learner asks for a structured summary of completed work, changes, checks run, open questions and anything still running, then compares it with the actual files.

## Core concepts

- **Check:** find out what is done, what proof exists and whether anything needs your attention; a status update alone does not show that a background process is still alive.
- **Decide:** answer one bounded question whose facts fit the screen, naming choice and scope; otherwise tell the session to wait.
- **Capture:** speak an idea or a correction aloud, read through the transcribed text, then send; type exact filenames out, since voice recognition can garble them.
- **Set a stopping point before leaving,** with the next permitted step defined and the host kept available.
- **Verify on return:** compare the summary with the real files or task status; save decisions in the progress file.
- **No constant checking required.**

## Frameworks and methods

Choose a task small enough to review on mobile, give it an explicit stop, review the list on the phone, then reconcile on return.

## Apply to icm-workshop

- **C3 desk:** adopt check, decide, capture as the phone vocabulary for the morning glance and Friday review; a "decide" is how a salesman or Jamie answers a ready-for-Jamie flag without opening a repo.
- **Boundary — gates are human:** a phone "decide" can tick a gate only when Jamie does it himself; the agent reads, never ticks.
- **Never:** nothing is sent to a client from a phone session; encode in the module's rules with the date and why.
