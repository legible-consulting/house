## Summary

A whiteboard session asking whether AI kills consulting firms. Jake argues the consulting model survives but its pricing model breaks: every firm sells some mix of advice, building and a warranty, and AI collapses the hours behind the first two while leaving the third untouched. He walks the three products across five kinds of consultancy (strategy, management and operations, technical, financial, HR), arguing that large firms carry headcount their new prices cannot support, while small firms can deliver strategy plus a technical solution with a small team. His main moat is opinion: deciding which build is right for a given client is a judgment AI cannot settle. A viewer leaves with a vocabulary for what to sell, what to stop billing by the hour, and where a small AI-native consultancy can outcompete a big one.

## Core concepts

- **Pricing model, not consulting model:** clients still want the deliverable; what breaks is charging for hours and headcount once AI produces the research, decks and first drafts faster.
- **Advisory product:** research, strategy decks and training billed by the hour, backed by the firm's stored know-how; those playbooks could be handed to an AI, so billable time shrinks.
- **Build product:** agencies and integrators earn on the deliverable and the hours to make it; AI makes first drafts of sites and CRMs nearly free, so value moves to what comes after the first draft.
- **Warranty product:** a board hires a big firm so that responsibility is shared and no single executive takes the fall. AI cannot absorb blame, echoing an old IBM line that a computer cannot be held accountable. More staff does not make this guarantee stronger.
- **Opinion as moat:** AI lowered the cost of writing software, not the cost of choosing the right software or getting people to use it. The same deliverable needs different builds for different clients.
- **Small beats big on governance:** a small firm can build compliance in from day one; a large one has to retrofit AI governance across thousands of staff. Regulation still favours big players in finance and HR.
- **Enterprise brain context packages:** a queryable model of an organisation (org chart, ownership, procedures) that tells staff what to do, which he sees as the next management-consulting product.

## Frameworks and methods

1. **Three products by five areas:** for each consulting area (strategy, management and operations, technical, financial, HR), ask what advice, building and warranty look like and which one AI compresses.
2. **The calculator test:** one deliverable, three clients, three right answers. A space agency wants a low-level language it can fully control; a typical company wants something that scales; a hardware firm dictates the software and cares about the device. The value is the per-client decision.
3. **Real decision at Eduba:** the CTO preferred C# with Blazor for one codebase; the product designer was far more fluent in React. They chose React, weighing team skill and speed over technical purity, which he presents as productised opinion.
4. **Strategy plus technical:** a small strategy firm can now add a technical layer (setting the client up with Claude or Codex inside their own business), which used to need a large team.
