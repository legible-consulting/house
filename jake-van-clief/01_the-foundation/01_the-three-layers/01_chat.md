# 1.1 Chat

*The Foundation › Module 1: The Three Layers · Author: Jake Van Clief · Source: Skool video + text page · Length: ~6:30 (estimated from captions)*

## Summary

Layer one is plain chat, and the lesson uses it to teach three working ideas. The model supplies the intelligence while the surrounding app decides what it can reach; the AI sees only what is on its desk for this conversation; and every word in a prompt is an unanswered question that a file, a connection or the human must answer. The learner runs one real job (a client email reply), corrects it in plain words, and writes the corrections down, because that list seeds the next lesson's skill.

## Core concepts

- **Brain versus app:** capability depends on what the surrounding app lets the model open, run and save; the same model feels smarter when it can touch files.
- **The desk:** only what was typed, attached or remembered is visible. Missing means guessed; excess means it guesses which item you meant. This is why one chat per job is the rule and old chats leak earlier work.
- **Every word is a question:** even a tiny request leaves tone, audience, voice and promises unspecified. At layer one the human answers each time, which is the pain that skills and folders later remove.
- **Corrections are decisions:** each fix encodes something about voice, about what is actually known, or about the relationship, and a wrong factual promise costs more than a stiff sentence.
- **Memory is the app's choice:** built-in memory helps but does not give you a written, transferable process.

## Frameworks and methods

1. Open a fresh chat for the job.
2. Paste real material plus a short request.
3. Correct output in plain words until sendable.
4. Log each correction.
5. Bring the log to 1.2.
