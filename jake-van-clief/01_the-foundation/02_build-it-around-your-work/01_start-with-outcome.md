# 2.1 Start With the Outcome

*The Foundation › Module 2: Build It Around Your Work · Author: Jake Van Clief · Source: Skool video + text page · Length: ~4:40 (estimated from captions)*

## Summary

Before opening any tool, define who the work is for and what they must do next, then split effort using a rule of thumb: most of the work is data, questions and thinking, a middle share is tools that already exist, and only a small slice is the AI itself. Jake argues people reverse this, starting at the AI slice and wondering why output keeps going wrong. The lesson ends with a worked example (a weekly bookings table) that the next lessons build on.

## Core concepts

- **Sixty, thirty, ten:** roughly 60 percent knowing the data and the problem, 30 percent existing tools (calendar, spreadsheet, email), 10 percent AI. The numbers are deliberately rough; he notes an older technical split (code, routing, AI calls) that he now considers a less fundamental version.
- **Facts beat prose:** the "don't promise the room" correction from 1.1 cannot be fixed by better writing because the answer lives in a calendar.
- **Outcome as a decision aid:** output exists to help someone decide, so quality is measured in speed to decision, cost of the decision, and how people interact with it.
- **Framing shapes the build:** naming the job as clear date answers might lead you to fix the calendar first, not build an email writer (Weinberg, 1971).
- **Smallest thing first:** check what the chat, a project or a skill already does; add pieces only when files keep changing, steps repeat weekly or someone else must use it.

## Frameworks and methods

Four questions: who is this for, what must they do next, where does the information come from now, and what breaks and who feels it. Then assign the split. Worked example: a Monday bookings export becomes an owner table; the sixty is knowing pending bookings matter, the thirty is the export and spreadsheet, the ten is a short AI heads-up.

## Claims and evidence

- His own posting workflow splits the same way: sixty is knowing what to post from performance data, thirty is a scheduling tool, ten is AI scripts and captions — described, not shown.
