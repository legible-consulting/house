# 2.1 Choose Your Concept: Series Guide

*The Foundation › History and Concepts · Author: Jake Van Clief · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

A routing page for the Abstraction Series, the run of concept lessons (2.2 to 2.7) in this module. Instead of a linear course it poses six questions a learner might be stuck on and points each to one companion lesson. It also carries a short three-line exercise to bring into the folder walkthrough. It teaches nothing on its own; its value is as a map and as a statement of how the author wants the series framed (as tools for investigation, not forecasts).

## Core concepts

- **Question-first navigation:** each lesson is introduced by the problem it answers rather than by its topic, so the series can be entered from whichever question blocks the current build.
- **Interface versus machinery:** 2.2 is positioned as the way to separate what you see from what does the work.
- **Stored versus active context:** 2.3 is positioned as the lesson to read before deciding what goes in an instruction file.
- **Trends are prompts to investigate:** 2.4's framing says a technology trend is something to examine, not a forecast to rely on.
- **Case study, not product advice:** 2.5 uses an agent platform to show routing, tools, model access and channels; names and popularity counts are described as historical.
- **Heuristic, not law:** the 60/30/10 label in 2.7 is called a design heuristic, not a measured rule for every system.

## Frameworks and methods

1. The six question-to-lesson routes: what is underneath an AI tool (2.2), why the assistant loses the thread (2.3), where to build (2.4), what an agent platform provides (2.5), how a written spec becomes a video (2.6), how to examine an AI system (2.7).
2. A three-line exercise: state the problem you are solving, the context the task needs, and how you will know the result is usable; then carry those lines into the folder walkthrough (lesson 0.3).
