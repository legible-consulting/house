# 4.3 Pre-Build Planning and Prompt Sequencing

*AI Animations & Workflows › Module 4: Building Websites · Author: Jake Van Clief · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Before choosing a framework, decide who will read the site, what they should learn and which link they should click. The lesson turns that into a five-step sequence that ends in a reviewed requirements document, with no site code written until the plan is approved. It then reiterates that planning takes real effort and that a low prompt count does not prove the site is useful.

## Core concepts

- **Outcome first:** audience, learning and desired action give something concrete to build and check.
- **Observed versus assumed:** the AI separates what it saw from what it guessed, and unknowns are explicitly marked.
- **Saved brief as session handoff:** audience, content, references and design observations in one file give the next session something specific to read.
- **Boundaries up front:** first page, hosting target, technology preference and features deferred; accounts, payments and form processing are separate decisions.
- **Capped questions:** at most three, about choices that change the build, such as logo format, photography, approved copy or contact method.
- **Requirements document:** sections, colours, typography, spacing, asset paths and acceptance checks, including mobile layout and working links.
- **Replace vague adjectives with examples or observable choices.**

## Frameworks and methods

1. Inspect the starting point with a bounded task.
2. Save the brief.
3. Set boundaries.
4. Resolve key gaps with up to three questions.
5. Review and correct the requirements document before building.

After the first preview, update the brief with what changed and track unresolved decisions and revisions.

## Apply to icm-workshop

- **S3 proposal / D1 onboarding:** the five steps map to discovery then stage mapping in the workspace-builder walk; adopt the "three questions, mark unanswered explicitly" rule for the client questionnaire.
- **D2 SDLC:** the requirements document with acceptance checks is the artefact that sizes a ticket by tier; keep it as the declared input to build.
- **Boundary — gates are human:** "stop after the requirements document" is a human approval gate to keep as a checkbox.
