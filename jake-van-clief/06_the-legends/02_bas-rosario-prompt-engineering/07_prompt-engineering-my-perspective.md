# PROMPT ENGINEERING: My perspective

*THE LEGENDS › 2. Bas Rosario – Prompt Engineering · Author: Bas Rosario · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

A personal essay that closes the first series. Bas explains why he opened his classroom on rung one despite the claim that it is finished: building a library of prompting techniques showed him that they cluster by task, so a small map beats a long catalogue. He names what he thinks is overrated (hunting for the next formula) and underrated (simply naming the task), and offers a model-as-mirror view of bad outputs. He closes with his recommended first week and the limit of everything taught: it works only while you steer live.

## Core concepts

- **Map beats catalogue:** holding ten techniques and a map is better than two hundred destinations and none.
- **Overrated: the next formula.** The belief that a further technique will make the model behave misses the question of what the task is.
- **Underrated: the name.** Many disappointing outputs trace to not naming the task, or naming it but omitting what its shape requires.
- **Model as mirror:** the model returns what you asked, not what you meant, so gaps in your own thinking come back with confidence. The application note adds that a model can also misread a clear request or err on good input, so check supplied evidence and tools before choosing a repair.
- **Starting Monday:** take five real prompts, name and route them, fix failures by checking structure first, and keep the card visible.
- **The edge:** the techniques give a good answer when you are steering, not the same answer tomorrow or from someone else.

## Claims and evidence

- Most bad output is the mirror, not the model — stated from the author's experience — the page itself qualifies this as incomplete.
- A week on five real prompts is worth more than a year of collecting templates — opinion.
