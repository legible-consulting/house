# Class 3 of 3 : Checkpoints and convergence. 📚

*THE LEGENDS › 6. Bas Rosario - Graph Engineering · Author: Bas Rosario · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Class 3 asks where a person belongs in a parallel graph and how outputs from branches that never shared context should be combined. Bas argues disagreement between branches is proof the graph ran, not a failure. Gates belong where one artifact fans out and where branches fan in; between them the contracts hold. He contrasts delegating convergence to a "smoothing" branch (which silently averages differences with nobody deciding) with a human-run convergence designed in advance around a cross-read list, with every difference surfaced and ruled on by name.

## Core concepts

- **Two gate locations:** at fan-out (approval of the shared artifact protects every branch) and at fan-in (convergence, the last read before anything leaves).
- **Merge hides, decide resolves:** a smoothing agent holds no stakes and settles differences silently in nobody's favour; it also cannot see ground that no draft covered.
- **Branch disagreement is expected:** branches that never spoke cannot agree on what the sheet did not decide.
- **Stakes belong to a person:** the machine may write well, but whoever holds the consequences holds the convergence.
- **Facts go up, not sideways:** a factual contradiction means the shared artifact failed; fix it at the top and rerun affected branches.

## Frameworks and methods

1. Before running, write a short cross-read list of questions the whole must answer identically (audience, lead point, next action).
2. When drafts return, read each alone against its own contract first.
3. Then read side by side against the list, listing every difference before fixing any.
4. Rule on each difference in one of three ways: pick one with a reason, assign the overlap one owner, or name the gap and assign it an author.
5. Factual conflicts go through the top and trigger reruns.
The exercise: circle the two gate points on your graph, write five or fewer cross-read questions and decide in advance how differences get ruled.

## Claims and evidence

- Questions invented after the drafts arrive tend to defend the favourite draft — reasoned assertion, no data.

## Apply to icm-workshop

- **Reinforces "gates are human" and rejects an automated merge.** Convergence is a decision, so a merge-agent or consolidating orchestrator is exactly what the "never build an orchestrator" boundary excludes. Where Jamie has fan-in (a proposal assembled from research, scope and quote in S3; the monthly close in C3 reading many client folders), the combining read is a human gate, with a cross-read list in the stage contract.
- **S3 to S5 chain:** the proposal, quote and agreement must not contradict each other. A cross-read list (client, price, scope, language, dates) at the agreement gate would catch what a smoother would hide.
- **C3 Friday review:** treat the flat open-items list as a convergence reading and record differences before correcting any.
- **Adopt** the two-circle gate placement as a stage-mapping checklist item; **reject** any "consistency pass" delegated to a model without a human ruling.
