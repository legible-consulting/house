# LOOP ENGINEERING 📚 Series 4 Overview

*THE LEGENDS › 5. Bas Rosario - Loop Engineering · Author: Bas Rosario · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Bas opens rung four of his ladder: everything before it was single-pass (ask, answer, human judges, human says "again"). A loop moves that judging step into the system, so "again" turns into a call the system makes by checking your written criteria. The thesis is that acting is easy and evaluating is hard, so a loop is only as good as its evaluator. The overview sets up three classes (anatomy, stopping and guardrails, human checkpoints), a quick card and a personal perspective post, and asks you to count your own "agains" first.

## Core concepts

- **You are already the loop:** drafting, reading, saying "shorter", reading again and approving is a full act-evaluate-decide-repeat circuit with the human filling two of the four seats. The series automates the middle.
- **Repetition is not iteration:** running the model twice with no judgement in between is only a re-roll. Real iteration needs a verdict on whether the second try improved on the first.
- **The evaluator is the "done when" line promoted:** the success criteria from the earlier "room" series become something the system checks on its own output rather than something a person eyeballs.
- **Hard signal vs soft signal:** wherever a test, a number or a build result exists, loops can run a long way unaided. Where "good" is a matter of feel, the evaluator has to be a rubric or a person, and pretending otherwise means polishing the wrong thing with full confidence.
- **Two shapes of loop:** research loops spread outward and end at saturation; refinement loops close in on a fixed target and end once the standard is reached or improvements become marginal. Mixing them produces premature "thorough" research or endless "careful" polish.
- **The ladder is ordered on purpose:** rooms (series 2) and memory (series 3) are prerequisites, because without memory a loop forgets what it already tried, and without a room it cannot be trusted to make an attempt at all.

## Claims and evidence

- The ordering of rungs is strict and each is a dependency — argued from the earlier series, not demonstrated here — framing claim.
- Bas cites his own memory system (762 memories, still human-validated one call at a time) as the confession that led into this rung — anecdote, self-reported.

- **Unattended runs:** the goals allow only three unattended jobs (estate drift check, deadline sweep, research recheck). Those are the only places a self-judging loop would run without Jamie; the research recheck is the natural research-shape loop with a saturation and budget rule.
