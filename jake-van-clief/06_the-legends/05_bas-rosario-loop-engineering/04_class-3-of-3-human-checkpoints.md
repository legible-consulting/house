# Class 3 of 3 : Human checkpoints. 📚

*THE LEGENDS › 5. Bas Rosario - Loop Engineering · Author: Bas Rosario · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Bas answers where the human belongs in a loop. His rule is to automate what is checkable and checkpoint what is subjective or irreversible. He compares three placements: the human inside every cycle (slow, moving target), the human absent (checks pass but nothing guards consequences), and one designed checkpoint after all countable checks and before anything leaves the building. He then spells out what a good checkpoint contains and closes by noting the learner has started designing flow between workers, which leads to the next series.

## Core concepts

- **Two kinds of soft judgment:** quality that will not convert to checks (taste, tone, meaning) and stakes, meaning any step that cannot be undone, such as sending, publishing, deleting, spending or promising. The person who carries the consequences owns the checkpoint.
- **Wrong placement one: everywhere:** reading every attempt ties the loop to your own availability, and new opinions each round keep shifting the target. It is single-pass work with extra steps.
- **Wrong placement two: nowhere:** a loop that sends on all-pass can ship something every check approved but nobody read. Checks verify, they do not hold stakes.
- **Right placement:** one read of the best attempt, after everything countable, before the irreversible step.
- **Fix notes become checks:** if the same correction is named twice, it is a missing check; write it into the list so the loop needs the human less. The aim is to spend the human where only they count, not to remove them.

## Frameworks and methods

A checkpoint is designed in four parts. Placement: after the automated checks, before the irreversible step, and outside the revision cycle. Handback: the best attempt together with each check result and any fixes made. One question written beforehand, asking for a go or a single change, instead of an open invitation to comment. Two exits: approve, or send back with one stated reason.

Exercise: write the checkpoint as four short lines covering what must never happen without you, what reaches you, the question you ask, and the exits you allow. Run the whole circuit once, count how many times you read (it should be once) and see whether your fix note became a check.

## Claims and evidence

- Open-ended feedback restarts the treadmill, a written question ends it — reasoned argument, no data.
