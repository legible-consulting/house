# Class 2 of 3 : Stop conditions and guardrails📚

*THE LEGENDS › 5. Bas Rosario - Loop Engineering · Author: Bas Rosario · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Class 2 covers the third decide outcome, stop, and the fences that keep loops from failing at their endings. Bas separates research loops (divergent, finish at saturation) from refinement loops (convergent, finish at the standard or when gains go marginal), then shows that "keep improving until it cannot be improved" is a treadmill, not a stop condition. He gives three guardrails (budget, fixed target, saturation rule) and a one-question test for naming a loop's shape.

## Core concepts

- **Done means different things per shape:** research cannot define "everything" in advance, so it stops when a full pass adds nothing new. Refinement has a written target, so it stops when the target is met.
- **Failure modes are endings gone wrong:** research quits too early and presents a shallow pass as complete; refinement polishes forever or drifts away from what an earlier attempt got right.
- **Four doors to close in a refinement ending:** done (all checks pass), marginal (an attempt moves no check from fail to pass), budget (a hard attempt ceiling), and fixed target (judge every attempt against the original checks, never the previous attempt).
- **Budget is insurance, not quality:** set before running, because "one more pass" always feels cheap inside the loop. When the budget ends the loop, deliver the best attempt rather than the last one.
- **Drift never announces itself:** judging each attempt only against its predecessor makes a random walk that feels like progress.

## Frameworks and methods

- **Shape test:** is there a fixed target you could write checks for in advance? Yes means refinement; if the answer is what you are searching for, it is research. A task needing both (gather then polish) is two loops, research feeding refinement, split rather than merged.
- **Saturation rule:** after each research pass, record what was new; stop when that comes back empty twice in a row, and still give research a budget.
- **Comparison table (described):** two rows, research and refinement, with columns for job, done-condition and characteristic failure.

## Claims and evidence

- Bas bets most people's budget is three and that they have never written one down — anecdotal guess in the closing comment prompt.

## Apply to icm-workshop

- **Unattended runs:** the estate drift check, deadline sweep and research recheck are the only allowed unattended jobs. The research recheck (O1 dated verdicts with recheck date) is a divergent loop and should carry saturation plus a budget; the drift check is convergent. Write both rules in each job's own folder so it starts cold.
- **Reject open-ended endings in any contract:** "until I am happy" is not an ending. Every stage that revises should state its attempt limit and the best-attempt handback.
- **Gates are human:** when a budget stops a loop, the handback goes to a human gate; it does not auto-advance a stage.
- **Never build an orchestrator:** the stop rules belong in the stage's plain-language contract, not in a retry script.
