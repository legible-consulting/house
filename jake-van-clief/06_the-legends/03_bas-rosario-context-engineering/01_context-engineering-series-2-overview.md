# CONTEXT ENGINEERING Series 2 Overview

*THE LEGENDS › 3. Bas Rosario – Context Engineering · Author: Bas Rosario · Source: text lesson*

## Summary

Bas moves from the instruction (rung one) to everything the model can see when it reads that instruction. His thesis is that the same prompt gives different answers on different days mostly because the surrounding material changed, and that "lucky" is simply context nobody wrote down. He previews three classes on window allocation, examples, and boundaries, plus a quick card and perspective essay. The homework is a missing-context audit of one past successful prompt.

## Core concepts

- **Context versus prompt:** the prompt states the job; context carries its sources, examples and boundaries into the next attempt or a teammate's workspace. This improves consistency but promises neither identical nor correct output.
- **Why Tuesday worked and Thursday did not:** in the illustration, the earlier update, a rejected draft and a named audience were all visible on Tuesday and gone in a fresh window.
- **Seven-part inventory of what the model should see:** the prompt, the objective, tools to use, tools not to use, what good looks like, the environment, and what bad looks like.
- **Bad examples as fences:** a good example points toward a target; a short "not like this" marks the edge and often does more than a paragraph of positive description.
- **Other factors acknowledged:** model settings, tool behaviour and ordinary variation also change outputs, so context is one cause among several.

## Frameworks and methods

1. Class 1: treat the window as designed space, choosing what is needed and leaving room for the answer.
2. Class 2: replace vague style words with a few tested examples, including labelled rejects.
3. Class 3: specify permitted and forbidden tools, the environment, and written success criteria.
4. Entry exercise: write down everything the model could see on a past good run and mark what a cold start would lack.

## Apply to icm-workshop

- **Adopt** the missing-context audit as the cold-start test for every module: open the module folder with no memory and list what a fresh session lacks. This operationalises the boundary that every module starts cold from its own folder.
- **Adapt** the seven-part list as a checklist for what a module contract plus shared core must cover; the objective, good and bad examples and environment belong in the module, while voice, terms and house rules live once in C1.
- The "luck is unwritten context" idea supports the goals file's rule that a rule carries its own why and date: write down what made a result good.
